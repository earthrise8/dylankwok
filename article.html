Getting Your First Rocketry Certification

By Dylan Kwok

High-power rocketry certifications are a crucial milestone for any student interested in aerospace. In this post, I'll walk through what to expect during the Level 1 certification process, common mistakes to avoid, and how to prepare your rocket for success.

Level 1 Certification is an amazing project all people should try. It's surprisingly simple, just like getting a driver's license. Let's get into it.

Understanding the Basics

Put simply, all you gotta do is launch a rocket with an H or I motor, fly straight and true, and then recover the rocket without significant damage. Now the question is: which rocket to launch?

I'd recommend going with a kit for your first flight, as many of the techniques required for building higher power rockets are significantly different than low and mid power. If you haven't ever built your own rocket before, please don't make your L1 certification your first launch. In rocketry, you always assume that you can do something easily, only to find out you've made a stupid mistake. Make those stupid mistakes early, with cheaper rockets, rather than with $50 motors.

Choosing Your Rocket

For the kit you choose, I would recommend going with a big rocket. Reason: Big rockets will fly lower. This will increase your chance of being able to see the rocket through the entire launch, and therefore increase your chances of recovery.

Ideally, you want a rocket with a 38mm motor mount, as it will allow the rocket to support a wider variety of motor sizes, and you also have the option to use an adapter to launch with smaller motors.

Pre-Launch Preparation

So, you've got your rocket ordered and assembled. Hopefully, you've managed to find the simulation file that comes with the kit, and have loaded it into simulation software to make sure the rocket will fly well. If you don't know how to do that, check out this guide on rocket simulations.

As a resident of the United States, you're probably going to be looking for a local launch site from NAR or Tripoli. This guide is for NAR certification, because that is the process I went through. You should be able to use the map to find local organizations and find people to launch with.

Certification Requirements

Before you launch, you will need to get a NAR membership.

If you are a highschooler, you will need to pass the online safety test. After that, you should get a certificate of completion, which you should print out and bring with you to the launch site.

You should also print out the Level 1 Certification Form, which is necessary for certification.
